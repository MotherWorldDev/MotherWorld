Recorded species

MotherWorld shows species recorded in public biodiversity datasets within its mapped regions. Sampling varies between places, groups and dates. These inventories are not complete lists of every species present, and occurrence counts are records, not population estimates. A missing record is not evidence of absence.

Land and lakes: GBIF

Regional inventories use the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF) occurrence API. Queries require coordinates and present occurrences, exclude GBIF geospatial issues, and include observation, specimen and material record types. They have no date cutoff unless the inventory says otherwise.

Names and higher taxonomy use the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy, 28 August 2023, matching the legacy species identifiers used by these queries. This name archive does not provide current IUCN status. Inventories built from a GBIF Species List download may include those categories when supplied. Unresolved names are identified explicitly.

GBIF brings together data from many contributing institutions. Dataset licenses include CC0, CC BY and CC BY-NC. Consult GBIF terms and agreements and the contributing datasets before reusing records; a regional aggregate does not change their licenses.

Marine regions: OBIS

Marine inventories use the Ocean Biodiversity Information System (OBIS) checklist API and its WoRMS-based taxonomy. All checklist partitions are read. Lower taxa are grouped under their parent species; taxa identified only above species level are excluded. OBIS quality control excludes dropped records by default.

Credit OBIS and its contributing data providers. See the OBIS access documentation and individual dataset licenses for reuse and citation requirements.

Species profiles and name lookup

Kingdom tabs, class and family filters, minimum record counts and sorting use the selected region's downloaded inventory. Names, taxonomy and identifiers are searched locally. The separate name lookup searches the GBIF Backbone for common or scientific names and keeps only matches in that inventory. Broad queries inspect the first 100 GBIF results; narrow the name when the lookup indicates additional results. A matching name does not add a species to a region's inventory.

Opening a species fetches a profile from the GBIF Species API and, for marine inventories, the WoRMS REST service. Available fields include common names, descriptions, taxonomy, habitat and other traits, reported distributions, synonyms and references. Marine profiles retain WoRMS taxonomy; GBIF supplements them only after an exact species-name and kingdom match. Source records can differ in coverage, dates and taxonomic treatment.

Trait qualifiers, units, references, review status and inheritance from a higher taxon are retained when supplied. Distribution and conservation reports describe the species across source datasets; they are separate from the selected region's occurrence counts. Conservation reports should not be treated as a current IUCN assessment unless the linked source establishes that. Missing information is left unavailable.

Profiles show a selection of available source records, with links to the full records. Photographs come from matching GBIF occurrences and retain each image's individual credit, licence and source link. Only images explicitly marked CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0 or public domain are shown. Photos may have been recorded elsewhere in the species' range. The GBIF image cache supplies resized images, loaded one at a time when visible.

Profile details are requested only when a species is opened and cached temporarily in the browser session. Provider failures leave the regional inventory usable and offer a retry. Common-name lookup contacts GBIF only when requested. Neither operation requires a user account or includes local project credentials.

Corrected species query boundaries

Seven remote land regions use maintained query footprints because the simplified globe outlines omit small islands or ice-free areas, and the older Brazilian island outlines have location errors. Adélie Land, Ellsworth Land, South Antarctic Peninsula, South China Sea Islands and San Félix–San Ambrosio use the complete multipart geometry from RESOLVE Ecoregions 2017.

Trindade–Martin Vaz and St. Peter and St. Paul use the island-group perimeters from the coordinate tables in Brazilian Decree 8,400/2015. These maritime baseline envelopes can include small waters between islets; they are query footprints, not biological range boundaries or land-only coastlines. The globe still displays its simplified ecoregion outlines, which can differ from these corrected query areas. Each affected inventory identifies its geometry source and reference.

The corrections retain PRESENT occurrence status, georeferenced records, the existing observation/specimen record types and exclusion of records flagged with geospatial issues. No absence records, unrestricted nearby search, or relaxed quality filter is used to fill a region.

Boundaries, counts and refreshes

Land queries normally use RESOLVE Ecoregions 2017, with the island corrections described above; marine queries use MEOW boundaries and lakes use HydroLAKES. The selected geometry is further simplified by at most 0.01 degrees and split where necessary to fit API limits. Coastal and boundary records can therefore differ from a full-resolution spatial analysis; records exactly on a split boundary may appear in both adjacent queries.

Each inventory stores its source, generation date, query fingerprint, filters, taxonomy provenance and record counts. IUCN labels are shown only when supplied by the source and should not be treated as a current conservation assessment. For such use, consult the IUCN Red List directly.

The earlier One Earth editorial material continues to inform Climate and Threats, with references shown in those tabs. The Species tab uses the newer occurrence inventories.
